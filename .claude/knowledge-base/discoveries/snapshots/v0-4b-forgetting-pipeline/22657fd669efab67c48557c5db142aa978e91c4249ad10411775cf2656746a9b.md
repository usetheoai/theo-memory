# TinyLFU: A Highly Efficient Cache Admission Policy (Einziger, Friedman, Manes — arXiv 1512.00727) — extracted 2026-05-26

(1) Admission mechanism: TinyLFU is a frequency-based cache ADMISSION policy (not pure eviction). On every reference, the proposed item is compared against the eviction candidate; admission is decided based on recent access frequency history.
(2) Long-tail handling: works well on "skewed access distributions"; in DYNAMIC distributions a smart eviction policy still matters. W-TinyLFU uses a small LRU window (typically 1%) as a "second chance" for new arrivals before the frequency filter rejects them — this is the design that proves most robust across both skewed and shifting workloads.
(3) Eviction pairings: TinyLFU pairs with various replacement policies as an admission filter. W-TinyLFU = window cache (LRU) + main cache (under TinyLFU admission control).
(4) Hit-ratio claim: W-TinyLFU obtains "equal or better hit-ratios than other state of the art replacement policies" across all tested traces. In static skewed distributions, post-admission the difference between eviction policies becomes marginal — even naive eviction matches true LFU.

Limitation flagged: On CDN-style workloads with churning long-tail content (CDN1, CDN2 traces in the paper), TinyLFU has HIGHER miss ratios than LRU and 2Q — strict frequency filter can be too aggressive when popularity shifts.

Context for v0-4b: W-TinyLFU is the closest competitor to TheMemorys long-tail protection multiplier idea, but operates at the ADMISSION boundary, not via in-store score multipliers. TheMemorys D4 multiplier is more flexible (applied at scoring time, not admission time) and works for the persistent-memory case where every memory IS admitted; eviction decides who stays.
